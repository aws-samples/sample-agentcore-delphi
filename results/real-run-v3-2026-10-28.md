# Delphi real-model run, v3 (10 models, tiny to frontier) — 2026-10-28

- **Pre-registered** in `results/preregistration-v3-2026-10-28.md` before any v3 call.
- **Questions:** the same 1,000 v2 questions and split (307 test).
- **Pool:** ten arms across seven families, with a 180× spread in cost per query.
- **Spend:** v3 cost **$9.51**. The whole run (v1 + v2 + v3) cost **$13.64 of the $100 cap**. The judge took $9.80 of v3's $12.46 cache total, at its assumed upper-bound price.
- **Sampling:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 ran at its provider-default sampling, because it rejects `temperature`. Every other arm ran at temperature 0.
- **Interruption:** the run crashed once at 7,756/10,000 on a thread race in my cap check. I fixed it and resumed from the cache; no answer was paid for twice.

## Headline

**The frontier model was not the best model on these questions.** Gemma 3 27B, an open-weight model at about $0.00004 per query, matched or beat Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $0.0020 per query, roughly 50× more. That one fact decides the pre-registered results:

| test, lenient (primary) | result |
|---|---|
| oracle gate (≥ 3.0) | **2.93 test: FAIL** (just); 3.09 full set: pass |
| **H3** learnability (capture ≥ 0.60) | **0.737 PASS** |
| **H2** router superior to every model (Holm) | **FAIL**: beats 7 of 10 significantly; vs DeepSeek V3.2 +1.17 (CI −0.65 to +3.06) and vs Gemma 27B +1.21 (CI −0.55 to +2.96) not significant |
| **H1** cost-aware router vs best single (Gemma 27B) | **FAIL**: the best single model is already among the cheapest, so the router stays on it (cost ratio 1.02) |

**Descriptive, not pre-registered, and labelled as such: against the frontier model.** The quality router scores **97.95 vs Claude's 95.77** at **$0.000349 vs $0.001942 per query, 82% cheaper**. By the design's own non-inferiority test it would pass, with lower bound +0.10 and cost ratio 0.18. This is a real result on held-out data, but Claude is not the "best single model" the pre-registration names, so it is not an H1 pass.

Tiny models fail in visible ways: Gemma 3 4B picked "Ladon" for the Hydra, and Qwen3 32B answered 33.67 for an average of 37.

On train, the best single model was **Gemma 3 27B**, which is what H1 and the cost-aware selection use. On test, DeepSeek edges it, 96.78 vs 96.74.

## Phase 2: where the quality router sends questions (test, lenient, knn family)

| domain | router | best single there | routed to |
|---|---|---|---|
| prophecy | **95.4** | DeepSeek 95.2 | Gemma 27B 28, DeepSeek 30, Claude 13 |
| facts | 100.0 | (many tie at 100) | Nova Lite 74, Nova Pro 3 |
| reckoning | 100.0 | Nova Lite 100 | Nova Lite 80 |
| lore | 96.2 | Gemma 27B / Nova Pro / Claude 98.7 | mostly Nova Lite (50) |

The router learned the useful structure:
- It **pays for big models only on prophecy**, the one domain where they matter.
- It **sends arithmetic and facts to Nova Lite**, which costs $0.000023.

It loses about 2.5 points on lore by over-using Nova Lite. Selection was on capture, which is blind to cost, and the router still spends more than Gemma 27B ($0.000349 vs $0.000039).

**The pre-registered cost-aware router (lenient)** selected prototype at λ=0.5. In effect it is "Gemma 27B, with 47 questions sent to Nova Lite": quality 96.74 at $0.000040. It ties Gemma 27B on both quality and cost, so H1 fails.

## Strict scoring (secondary)

Under strict scoring everything passes:
- Gate: 15.37.
- H2: superior to all, +9.8 over Gemma 27B.
- H1: cost-aware router +4.70 quality at 75.3% lower cost, lower bound +1.60.

As in v1 and v2, though, strict mostly rewards terse answers, and no model reaches 50 on arithmetic. **Do not headline strict.**

## Audit

- **Questions every model misses (lenient): 0.**
- **Sensitivity without the ambiguous "temple columns" template** (n = 299): lenient H2 still fails, with weakest lower bound −1.57. Strict H2 still passes.
- **kNN routing latency:** P90 6.6 ms, against a 150 ms budget.

## What this means (toy questions, not a real workload)

1. **Model choice beat routing.** A cheap open-weight 27B model was as good as the frontier model on these questions at 2% of the price. The biggest saving on the table is "don't default to the frontier model", and you can see it without a router, just from the Phase 1 matrix.
2. **The router is a good *auditor* of where money matters.** It spends on large models for creative, constrained writing (prophecy) and on nothing else. It reaches 97.95, above every single model's mean, and 82% cheaper than Claude. That is descriptive, not pre-registered.
3. **The pre-registered claims still fail narrowly.**
   - H2: +1.2 points over the two best models, not significant at 307 test questions.
   - H1: the best model is already cheap, so there is nothing to save.
   - H3 passes in all three runs.
