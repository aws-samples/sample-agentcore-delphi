# Delphi real-model run, v2 (1,000 varied questions) — 2026-10-28

The questions, split, scoring and tests were pre-registered in `results/preregistration-v2-2026-10-28.md` before any v2 call. The cap was respected: **v2 cost $2.9468, and the total with v1 is $4.1311 of $10.** No calls were retried or repeated.

## Headline

**On v2, the cheapest model is the best single model under the primary (lenient) scoring.** Nova Lite scores 94.10 on test, ahead of Nova Pro (91.66) and Llama (91.50), at 1/13th of Nova Pro's price. That reverses v1 and changes what the router can do:

- **H1 (pre-registered primary): FAIL, and it cannot pass on this pool.** The design measures H1 against the best single model. That is now the cheapest model, so no router can be 30% cheaper than it. The cost-aware router correctly collapses to "always Nova Lite" (307/307), with quality 94.10 and cost ratio 1.000. *Finding: here the right answer is a single cheap model, not a router.*
- **H2 (quality): the router wins, by a small but statistically real margin.** The quality router scores **95.11 against Nova Lite's 94.10**. The design's superiority test (paired bootstrap with Holm-Bonferroni) finds it **superior to every single model**: vs Lite +1.01 (CI +0.10 to +2.15), vs Pro +3.45, vs Llama +3.62. This test is the design's own, but it was **not listed in my v2 pre-registration**, so treat it as a secondary result. The oracle gate, the design's go/no-go for pursuing H2, *failed* (gap 2.44 < 3.0). The gain is real but small, and it costs 4.6× Nova Lite ($0.000105 vs $0.000023 per query), though still less than Llama or Pro.
- **H3 (learnability): PASS** under both scorings. Capture is 0.643 lenient and 0.794 strict, against 0.60.

**Where the models differ** (this is what v1 lacked):

**Strict-scoring H1 pass is fragile.** The cost-aware router sends almost everything to Lite. It sends Llama only the 29 reckoning questions Llama answers tersely, and the bound clears −2 by just 0.09. Removing one ambiguous template (below) puts it exactly at −2.00, a fail.

## Audit (pre-registered) and one ground-truth problem

- **Questions every model misses (lenient): none**, so the pre-registered all-miss exclusion changes nothing.
- **Found in the miss review (not pre-registered):** the reckoning template "A temple has *a* columns on one side and *b* on the other. How many columns?" is **ambiguous**. Models reasonably treat it as a rectangle with shared corners ("49 + 44 − 2"). It accounts for **18 of the 31** lenient reckoning misses across 26 questions. That is my question-writing error. It was kept in the primary analysis as pre-registered.
- **Post-hoc sensitivity without those 26 questions:**
  - lenient quality router is still superior to all (lower bounds +0.10 / +0.87 / +1.10);
  - lenient H1 still fails on cost;
  - strict H1 falls to exactly −2.00, a fail.

## What it means (toy questions, not a real workload)

1. **Variety created real complementarity.** The form constraints and harder arithmetic made the models differ, and a learned router turned that into a small, significant quality gain over every single model. This is the first real-model evidence in this project that routing can beat the best single model on quality. The gain is about one point, and it costs more than the cheapest model.
2. **The cost story depends on the pool, not just the router.** On v1 the best model was expensive, so cost routing saved 74–89%. On v2 the cheapest model is also the best, so the right decision is "use Nova Lite" and H1 is unreachable by construction. A blog claim of "routing saves X%" needs to say which model it is measured against.
3. **Scoring still dominates.** Strict scoring flips the best single model (Llama), the gate (pass) and H1 (fragile pass). Lenient is the pre-registered primary scoring, for the reason given there.
